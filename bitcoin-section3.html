Timeline 03 / 09

I. The Wilderness

2010 — 2013
May 22, 2010

10,000 BTC
for two pizzas

Laszlo Hanyecz makes the first real-world commercial transaction. At the time: a fair deal. Bitcoin Pizza Day is celebrated every year not as a mistake, but as proof that Bitcoin had a price. The experiment was real.

≈ $0.0025 per BTC at the time
November 28, 2012

Halving I

Block reward drops from 50 BTC to 25 BTC. The first proof that the supply schedule holds. No authority voted on it. No announcement needed. The code ran. The issuance halved. The world barely noticed.

Block 210,000 ≈ $12 per BTC
April 2013

First Bubble.
First Crash.

Bitcoin reaches $266 — then collapses 83% in two days. The first of many obituaries is written. Bitcoin was declared dead for the first time. It was not the last time.

✝ Obituary #1 of many Peak $266 → trough $45
By end of 2013, BTC reaches $1,000 for the first time.
The internet notices. So does everyone else.

II. The Wars

2014 — 2017
February 2014

Mt. Gox
Collapses

The world's largest Bitcoin exchange halts withdrawals and files for bankruptcy. 850,000 BTC lost — approximately 6% of all bitcoin that will ever exist. Billions in customer funds, gone. Headlines declare Bitcoin finished. They are wrong. The protocol never missed a block. Only the custodian failed — a lesson the network would keep teaching until people listened.

✝ Obituary wave — 2014 $1,100 → $315 over 12 months
2015 — 2017

The Block
Size Wars

Bitcoin's most dangerous moment — not from outside, but from within. A coalition of large miners, companies, and developers push to increase the block size, arguing it's needed for scale. The counter-argument: bigger blocks centralise nodes, and centralised nodes are not Bitcoin. The battle is vicious. Careers end. Alliances shatter. The outcome defines what Bitcoin is for the next decade.

August 2017

SegWit Activates.
BCH Forks Off.

The nodes win. Segregated Witness activates on the Bitcoin network, laying the groundwork for Lightning. The large-block faction forks into Bitcoin Cash and leaves. Bitcoin's governance model is proven: you cannot co-opt a network where the users run the rules.

Milestone
December 2017

$20,000

The first mainstream mania. Every taxi driver, every family dinner, every news broadcast. Futures markets launch. Institutional eyes open. Then — as it always does — it collapses. Down 84% over the following year. The tourists leave. The builders stay.

ATH: $19,783 — Dec 17, 2017
July 9, 2016

Halving II

Block reward drops from 25 BTC to 12.5 BTC. The supply schedule holds again, indifferent to the chaos surrounding it.

Block 420,000 ≈ $650 per BTC
Bitcoin survived a major exchange collapse, a civil war,
and an 84% crash. It entered 2018 smaller — and harder.

III. The Institution

2018 — Present
May 11, 2020

Halving III

Block reward drops to 6.25 BTC. MicroStrategy announces its first Bitcoin purchase two months later. Paul Tudor Jones calls it the fastest horse. The institutional on-ramp opens — quietly at first, then all at once.

Block 630,000 ≈ $8,500 per BTC
May–June 2021

China Bans
Bitcoin. Again.

China outlaws Bitcoin mining. ~65% of global hashrate goes offline overnight. Every prior Bitcoin obituary is reprinted. The network difficulty adjusts. Within six months, hashrate fully recovers — now distributed across the US, Kazakhstan, and Canada. The ban stress-tested the network's geographic resilience. It passed.

✝ Obituary — widely published $64k → $29k in 60 days
September 2021

Legal Tender

El Salvador makes Bitcoin legal tender — the first sovereign nation to do so. The IMF objects. The bond markets sneer. The experiment runs anyway. Bitcoin becomes a geopolitical variable.

Milestone
April 20, 2024

Halving IV

Block reward drops to 3.125 BTC. Days earlier, the SEC approves the first US spot Bitcoin ETFs. BlackRock, Fidelity, and Invesco enter the market. In its first week, BlackRock's IBIT becomes one of the fastest ETFs to $10B AUM in history. The same institutions that dismissed Bitcoin are now its largest distribution channel.

Block 840,000 ≈ $63,000 per BTC at halving
January 10, 2024

Spot ETF
Approved

After a decade of rejections, the SEC approves 11 spot Bitcoin ETFs simultaneously. A 10-year regulatory war ends in a single ruling. Bitcoin is now accessible inside every brokerage account in America. The orange coin is in your 401(k) whether the system likes it or not.

Milestone
Bitcoin has been declared dead 430+ times since 2010.
The network has never missed a block. Make of that what you will.
