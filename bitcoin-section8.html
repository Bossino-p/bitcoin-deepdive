Bitcoin Standard
Read 2nd

The Bitcoin
Standard

Saifedean Ammous · 2018

"The most important book written about money in a generation."

Ammous builds the case for Bitcoin from first principles — tracing the history of money from commodity to fiat, explaining what makes something a good store of value, and showing why Bitcoin's fixed supply and proof-of-work make it the hardest money ever created. Confrontational, uncompromising, and essential. The book that converts economists.

Fiat Standard
Read 4th

The Fiat
Standard

Saifedean Ammous · 2021

"If Bitcoin Standard explains why Bitcoin, Fiat Standard explains what you're escaping."

The sequel that cuts deeper. Ammous dissects the fiat monetary system as a technology — how it operates, how it distorts economies, and what it has cost humanity in deferred time preference, debt accumulation, and the corruption of long-term thinking. More provocative than the first book. Deliberately so. Read it when you're ready to be angry.

Fiat critique Macro Advanced Best after → Bitcoin Standard
Layered Money
Read 1st

Layered
Money

Nik Bhatia · 2021

"The gentlest and most clarifying on-ramp to understanding how money actually works."

Bhatia explains monetary systems as hierarchical layers — gold, central bank reserves, commercial bank money, and now Bitcoin. Start here if you're new to monetary theory. The book demystifies how the dollar system actually functions before showing where Bitcoin fits as a new first layer. Accessible without being dumbed down. The book that makes everything else click.

Broken Money
Read 3rd

Broken
Money

Lyn Alden · 2023

"The most rigorous, evidence-based monetary history Bitcoin has yet produced."

Alden approaches money as a macro analyst — data-driven, historically grounded, intellectually honest about Bitcoin's open questions. She traces the full history of monetary systems, their failures, and why information asymmetry is the root of monetary dysfunction. Less ideological than Ammous, more empirical. The book that converts sceptics because it doesn't require you to accept premises — it shows you the evidence.
