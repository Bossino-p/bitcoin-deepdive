Self-Custody 06b / 09

Not your keys, not your coins.

Everyone learns this phrase. Far fewer learn what comes next. Holding your own keys is the full expression of Bitcoin's value proposition. It also makes you the single point of failure. Here is what that actually requires.

More bitcoin has been lost to human error than to any protocol attack in Bitcoin's history. Lost seed phrases. Forgotten passphrases. Hardware wallets in flooded basements. Estates where no heir knew the bitcoin existed. Hard drives thrown away before the price mattered.

James Howells famously lost 8,000 BTC on a discarded hard drive in a Newport landfill. Stefan Thomas lost the password to 7,002 BTC on an IronKey with two attempts remaining. These are not edge cases. They are the dominant failure mode.

The Mt. Gox lesson is "not your keys, not your coins." The self-custody lesson is equally unforgiving: your keys, your full responsibility. There is no Ctrl+Z. There is no support ticket. There is no court order that reverses a lost seed.

Risks & how to handle them

Your seed phrase is the master key to every bitcoin in a wallet. Anyone who has it controls your funds. If it's destroyed, your funds are gone. It must be both secure and recoverable. These two requirements are in direct tension — which is why most people get it wrong.

Paper degrades. House fires are real. Floods happen. The standard for serious holdings is metal backup — seed phrases stamped or engraved into stainless steel plates that survive fire, water, and time. Never store your seed phrase digitally — not in a photo, not in a notes app, not in a password manager, not in the cloud. Ever.

The rule At minimum two physical copies in two geographically separate locations. Metal for anything above a few hundred dollars in value. No digital copies under any circumstances.
Seed phrase storage
✓ Metal backup — Cryptosteel, Coldbit, or stamped steel plate
✓ Two copies in separate physical locations
✓ Fireproof/waterproof safe for at least one copy
✓ Passphrase (25th word) for an additional layer of encryption
✗ Photo on your phone — cloud-synced to iCloud or Google Photos
✗ Screenshot, notes app, email draft, or password manager
✗ Single paper copy in your home — fire, flood, theft
✗ Typed into any internet-connected device

The term comes from a famous security joke: no amount of cryptographic protection matters if someone holds a wrench to your head and demands your seed phrase. Bitcoin's cryptography is unbreakable. Humans are not.

The mitigation is not heroism — it is plausible deniability. A passphrase wallet (the "25th word") creates a completely separate wallet at the same seed. You keep a decoy wallet with a small balance at the base seed. Under duress, you hand over the decoy. Your real holdings sit behind the passphrase — which the attacker doesn't know exists.

Operational security also matters: don't publicly announce your holdings. The person who can't be targeted can't be coerced.

The rule Never disclose holdings publicly. Use a passphrase wallet for significant amounts. Maintain a credible decoy wallet with a small balance at the base seed.
Physical security
✓ Passphrase (25th word) — creates a hidden second wallet
✓ Decoy wallet with believable small balance at base seed
✓ Strict operational security — don't broadcast holdings
✓ Multisig — no single location holds enough to steal
✗ Posting wallet balances, hardware wallet unboxings publicly
✗ Telling people — even friends — the scale of your holdings
✗ Storing all bitcoin in a single-sig wallet with one seed location

Multisig (multi-signature) requires M of N keys to sign a transaction. The most common setup is 2-of-3: three keys exist, any two can spend. One key can be lost without losing funds. One key can be stolen without the thief being able to move anything.

The keys live in different locations — one at home, one at a trusted location, one with a collaborative custody provider or in a safety deposit box. An attacker must compromise two separate locations simultaneously. A fire destroys one location — you still have two keys. Multisig is the gold standard for any significant holding.

Tools: Sparrow Wallet for desktop, Nunchuk for mobile-first multisig, Unchained or Casa for collaborative custody with one key held by a professional co-signer.

The rule Single-sig is acceptable for small amounts. For significant holdings — anything you'd be devastated to lose — 2-of-3 multisig is the minimum acceptable standard.
2-of-3 multisig setup
🔑 Key 1 — Hardware wallet Home safe
🔑 Key 2 — Hardware wallet Safety deposit box
🔑 Key 3 — Co-signer Unchained / Casa
Any 2 of 3 keys required to spend
✓ 1 key lost → funds safe (use remaining 2 to recover)
✓ 1 key stolen → funds safe (thief has 1 of 3, needs 2)
✓ 1 location burns → funds safe (2 keys elsewhere)

If you die tomorrow, can your family access your bitcoin? Do they know it exists? Do they know what a seed phrase is? Do they know where it's stored? For most self-custody holders, the answer to all four questions is no.

Bitcoin inheritance is a unique problem. Unlike a bank account, there is no institution to contact, no probate process that unlocks funds, no customer service that can help. The seed phrase is the estate. If no one has it, the estate is gone.

The solution is a documented inheritance plan — instructions clear enough that a non-technical heir can follow them, secure enough that a thief finding them early can't act on them. These two requirements require careful design, not just a note in a drawer.

The rule Your heirs need to know bitcoin exists, where to find the seed, and how to use it. Document it. Review it every year. A lawyer familiar with digital assets should be involved for large estates.
Inheritance planning steps

Document existence

A trusted heir needs to know bitcoin exists and approximately how much. Not the seed — just that it's there.

Write access instructions

Step-by-step guide assuming zero technical knowledge. Where is the hardware wallet? What software is needed? What is a seed phrase?

Secure the instructions separately from the seed

Instructions alone are useless to a thief. Seed alone is inaccessible to a grieving family. Keep them separate with a clear trigger mechanism (e.g. sealed envelope with a lawyer).

Consider multisig as inheritance tool

A 2-of-3 where one key is held by a collaborative custody provider gives your heir a guided recovery path through that provider.

Review annually

Wallet addresses change. Hardware wallets get replaced. Amounts change. Your plan must stay current.

🔑

Being your own bank is not a metaphor. It carries the same weight of responsibility that running a bank carries — without the staff, the regulators, or the insurance. Bitcoin gives you unprecedented financial sovereignty. What you do with that responsibility is entirely yours. Set it up properly. Document it. Review it. The protocol will outlast all of us — make sure your holdings can too.
